# Universal Asset Analyzer — Complete User Guide

**What it is:** A personal investment research platform that gives you the same depth of analysis used by professional fund managers — live market data, AI-powered insights, and systematic scoring — all running privately on your own computer.

---

## How to Navigate

The toolbar at the top gives you access to every tool. Each one is independent — you can jump between them freely. Your watchlist, portfolio, and notes are saved automatically and persist between sessions.

---

## 1. Research

**What it does:** A complete dossier on any stock, US or Indian.

Type a ticker symbol (e.g. `AAPL`, `RELIANCE`, `TCS`) and you get everything about that company in one place:

- **Live price & chart** — the current price, today's change, and a historical price chart
- **Key numbers** — P/E ratio, market cap, revenue, earnings, margins, debt levels
- **Who owns it** — institutional investors (mutual funds, hedge funds), insider holdings (executives, directors), and recent insider buying or selling
- **Earnings history** — how the company has beaten or missed analyst expectations each quarter
- **Recent filings** — SEC regulatory documents (10-K annual reports, 10-Q quarterly updates, 8-K material events) for US stocks
- **Peer comparison** — how it ranks against its direct competitors on valuation and growth
- **AI Copilot** — a chat assistant at the bottom of the page that knows all the data it just loaded. Ask it anything: "Is this stock overvalued?", "What are the biggest risks?", "Summarise the last earnings call." It answers using only the real data shown on the page, not generic internet knowledge.

**For Indian stocks:** Type any NSE symbol (e.g. `INFY`, `HDFCBANK`) to get Indian-specific data including promoter holding, FII/DII ownership, 10-year historical ratios, and quarterly P&L from screener.in.

**When to use it:** Start here when you want to understand a specific company in depth.

## 2. Discover

**What it does:** Surfaces investment ideas you wouldn't have found on your own.

There are two ways to use this tool:

**News Signal Scanner**
Type a theme or leave the box blank and hit Scan. The tool reads hundreds of recent news headlines across Indian and global markets, then uses AI to extract concrete investment signals — which companies are affected, whether the impact is positive or negative, and how confident the signal is. For example, scanning "RBI rate cut" might surface banks as beneficiaries and highlight specific names seeing unusual activity.

You can filter signals by market (India / Global), by how strong the signal is (confidence level), or by category (earnings surprise, policy change, M&A, etc.).

**Fundamental Idea Scanner**
Switch to the Screener tab within Discover for a rapid filter of 500+ stocks by fundamental criteria — sector, profitability, valuation, and growth. This is a faster, simpler version of the full Screener tool.

**When to use it:** When you want the market to bring ideas to you, rather than researching companies you already know.

---

## 3. Themes

**What it does:** Deep research into an investment theme or industry, not just individual stocks.

Type any theme — "electric vehicles", "defence", "rare earth metals", "AI chips", "water infrastructure" — and the engine runs a structured 10-stage analysis:

1. **Future State** — Is this theme inevitable? What forces are driving it and over what time horizon?
2. **Dependency Chain** — What does this industry need to exist? (Raw materials → components → manufacturing → distribution → end user)
3. **Bottlenecks** — Where is the chokepoint? Which part of the chain is hardest to scale or replace?
4. **Supply & Demand** — Where is demand growing fastest, where is supply constrained, and what does that mean for pricing power?
5. **Commodities** — Which raw materials matter? Where are they concentrated geographically, and what happens if access is disrupted?
6. **Policy** — Government spending, regulations, subsidies, and trade policy that help or hurt the theme
7. **Hidden Winners** — Tier 2 and Tier 3 companies that benefit indirectly but are less obvious and potentially less crowded
8. **Risk Factors** — What could go wrong? Technology displacement, policy reversal, commodity crashes
9. **Entry Points** — Timing signals: when does the risk/reward look most attractive?
10. **Stock List** — Specific companies to investigate further, with a brief rationale for each

**When to use it:** When you have a macro view ("I think defence spending is going up") and want to translate that into specific investable ideas with rigour.

---

## 4. Compare

**What it does:** Side-by-side analysis of up to 5 stocks so you can pick the best one.

Add the tickers you want to compare. The tool lines them up across every major financial dimension:

- **Valuation** — Is it cheap or expensive relative to earnings, cash flow, and book value?
- **Growth** — How fast are revenues and profits growing, historically and projected?
- **Quality** — How efficiently does the business use its capital? How healthy is the balance sheet?
- **Momentum** — Has the stock been going up or down recently relative to peers?
- **Profitability** — Margins, return on equity, free cash flow generation

At the bottom, the AI gives a structured verdict: which stock wins on each dimension, which is the overall best risk/reward, and what the key differentiators are between them.

**Example use:** You want to invest in Indian private sector banks. Add HDFCBANK, ICICIBANK, KOTAKBANK, AXISBANK and compare them. The tool tells you which is cheapest, which has the best growth, and which the AI considers the most attractive on balance.

**When to use it:** When you've narrowed down to a shortlist and need to make a final decision between similar options.

---

## 5. Watchlist

**What it does:** Your personal tracking list for stocks you're interested in but haven't bought yet.

Add any ticker with one click from the Research page, or directly from the Watchlist page. For each stock you can set:

- **Price alert (target)** — Get notified when the stock reaches a price you want to buy at
- **Drop alert** — Get notified if the stock falls by a percentage you define (e.g. "alert me if this drops 10%")
- **Research notes** — A private text field to record your thesis, the price levels you're watching, upcoming catalysts, or anything else you want to remember

The AI Digest button at the top runs an AI summary across your entire watchlist — it looks at every stock's current scores, momentum, and fundamentals, then gives you a concise briefing on what deserves your attention today and any action items.

**When to use it:** For tracking ideas you're considering, monitoring stocks you follow, or keeping notes on your investment thinking before you commit capital.

---

## 6. Portfolio

**What it does:** Tracks your actual holdings with real-time profit and loss.

Add each position you hold: the stock symbol, how many shares, what you paid per share, and the currency. The tool then shows you:

- **Current value** — what each position is worth right now
- **Unrealised P&L** — how much you're up or down on each position, in both rupees/dollars and percentage
- **Cost basis** — your average purchase price across multiple buys
- **Allocation breakdown** — what percentage of your portfolio is in each stock, sector, and geography
- **Portfolio-level return** — overall gain or loss across everything you hold

**When to use it:** To see your current positions in one place and understand exactly where you stand financially across your investments.

## 7. Screener

**What it does:** A filter that lets you find stocks matching specific financial criteria from a universe of 1,000+ companies.

You can screen in two ways:

**Manual filters:** Use the dropdowns and sliders to set criteria — for example: sector = Technology, P/E ratio below 25, revenue growth above 15%, positive free cash flow. The screener instantly shows you every stock that qualifies.

**Natural language:** Describe what you want in plain English in the text box — "profitable small-cap consumer companies with low debt and growing dividends" — and the AI translates your description into the correct financial filters automatically.

You can sort results by any metric and click any row to jump straight to that company's full Research page.

The screener covers both US stocks (S&P 500 and broader universe) and global equities. It scores each stock on a composite quality-value-growth scale so you can rank results by overall attractiveness, not just individual metrics.

**When to use it:** When you want to generate a list of candidates — "which stocks meet my criteria?" — rather than researching a company you already know.

## 8. DCF (Valuation Calculator)

**What it does:** Estimates what a company is *actually worth* based on its future cash flows.

DCF stands for Discounted Cash Flow — the most widely used professional method for valuing a business. The idea is simple: a company is worth the sum of all the cash it will generate in the future, adjusted for the fact that money today is worth more than money in the future.

Type a ticker and the tool pre-fills the model with the company's real financial data. You then adjust three key assumptions:

- **Revenue growth rate** — how fast you expect the company to grow its sales
- **Free cash flow margin** — what percentage of revenue turns into actual cash profit
- **Discount rate** — your required rate of return (typically 8–12% for equities)

The tool shows you the resulting intrinsic value per share and compares it to the current price — giving you a margin of safety calculation (how much room there is between what you're paying and what the business is worth).

You can also run a sensitivity table — seeing how the valuation changes across a range of growth and margin assumptions, so you understand how sensitive the conclusion is to your key assumptions.

**When to use it:** When you've decided you like a company and want to answer the final question: "Is the current stock price a fair price to pay?"

## 9. Deep Research

**What it does:** A full investment committee-style report generated by a network of specialised AI agents working in parallel.

This is the most thorough analysis tool in the platform. When you run it on a stock, nine separate AI investigators simultaneously analyse different dimensions of the company:

| Agent | What it examines |
|---|---|
| Fundamental Analyst | Revenue trends, margin direction, earnings quality |
| Balance Sheet Analyst | Debt levels, liquidity, financial health |
| Valuation Analyst | Multiple valuation methods — DCF, comparables, sum-of-parts |
| Momentum Analyst | Price trends, relative strength, volume patterns |
| Insider Activity Analyst | Recent buying and selling by executives and directors |
| Earnings Quality Analyst | Are the reported profits real and sustainable? |
| Catalyst Analyst | Upcoming events that could move the stock |
| Risk Analyst | Key risks — operational, financial, sector-specific |
| India/Sector Analyst | Industry dynamics and competitive positioning |

After all nine agents complete their investigation, a synthesis layer assembles a full IC report containing:

- **Signal detection** — what the data is flagging (positive and negative)
- **Bull case, Bear case, Base case** — three structured scenarios with the key drivers of each
- **Variant perception** — where this analysis might differ from market consensus
- **Valuation range** — a composite intrinsic value estimate with upside/downside to current price
- **Monitorables** — the 5–8 things to watch that would change the investment case
- **Run Hot/Cold** — whether the stock is trading at an extreme relative to its own history

The report streams in live as each agent finishes — you see progress in real time.

**When to use it:** Before making a significant investment decision. This is the "write the full memo" tool — use it when you want to stress-test an idea with the same rigour a professional analyst would apply.

## 10. Quant Engine

**What it does:** Scores a universe of stocks systematically using mathematical models, removing emotion from the process.

Rather than analysing one company at a time, the Quant Engine scores an entire market (e.g. all 50 Nifty stocks) simultaneously across 10 factors and ranks them.

**The 10 scoring factors:**

| Factor | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Momentum | Is the price trending up relative to its peers? |
| Quality | How efficiently does the business generate returns? |
| Value | Is it cheap relative to its earnings and cash flows? |
| Low Volatility | Has it been more stable than peers (less risky)? |
| Earnings Revision | Are analysts upgrading or downgrading their forecasts? |
| Regime | What market environment are we in — bull, bear, ranging, or recovery? |
| Forecast | What do the model's 30/90/180-day return forecasts say? |
| Monte Carlo Value | What does a probabilistic DCF model say the stock is worth? |
| Kelly Fraction | How much of your portfolio should you allocate given the risk/reward? |
| Composite | The combined weighted score across all factors |

**The three buttons:**
- **Load Scorecard** — reads the most recent saved results (fast, no new computation)
- **Run Engine (fast)** — recalculates scores using the latest prices (takes ~2 minutes)
- **Full Run + Forecasts** — recalculates everything including machine-learning return forecasts (takes ~5 minutes)

**Reading the results:**
Each stock gets a signal: **Strong Buy**, **Buy**, **Hold**, **Sell**, or **Strong Sell**. Click any row to see the full mathematical working — exactly why the model scored that company the way it did, including the regime assessment, the valuation range from Monte Carlo, and the individual factor breakdown.

The **Live OOS Validation** panel at the top shows how well the model's past predictions have matched actual returns — a measure of how much to trust the scores.

**When to use it:** As a systematic starting point. Run the engine weekly on a universe you care about to get a ranked list of the most and least attractive stocks by the numbers. Then use Research and Deep Research to dig into the top-ranked names.

## How the Tools Work Together

A typical workflow might look like this:

1. **Quant Engine** → Run it on Nifty 50. See which stocks rank highest this week.
2. **Research** → Open the top 3 ranked stocks. Review their fundamentals and recent filings.
3. **Compare** → Put the top 3 side by side. See which one wins across the key dimensions.
4. **Themes** → If one stock is in a theme you believe in (e.g. defence), run a Themes analysis to validate the macro tailwind.
5. **DCF** → Plug the winner into the DCF to check that the current price makes sense.
6. **Deep Research** → Run the full IC report before committing capital.
7. **Watchlist** → Add it to your watchlist with a price alert at your target entry.
8. **Portfolio** → Once you buy, add it to Portfolio to track your P&L.

## AI Features Note

The Research Copilot, Discover signals, Deep Research agents, Themes analysis, and Watchlist Digest run on Claude via the Anthropic API, using your own API key (enter it once at /settings; it is stored on this machine). When these features run, the prompt — company data and, where relevant, portfolio context — is sent to api.anthropic.com; your database itself never leaves your computer, and every number on every page is computed locally. If these features show "analysis unavailable", add your Anthropic API key in Settings.
