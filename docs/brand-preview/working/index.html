◆ asset/analyzer WORKING PAPER No. 2 · IDENTITY · NOTHING HEREIN IS IMPLEMENTED

Ink is earned.
The rest is pencil.

Every terminal on earth prints a guess in the same ink as a fact. A price, a fair value, a model's hunch — identical pixels, identical conviction. UAA is the instrument that stopped doing that. It prints what it knows at the strength it knows it, and keeps its working in the margin, where you can check it.

this page is set in its own system: four inks, one pencil, no rounded corners.

the margin you are reading is not decoration. it is the identity.
§1 · PREMISE

A screen that admits what it knows

A price is a fact. A composite score is an argument. A twelve-month forecast is a bet. Software renders all three in identical type and calls it design. It is closer to lying by typesetting.

UAA cannot get away with that, because UAA actually knows better. Confidence runs through this codebase the way rebar runs through concrete: eighty-odd files in lib/ carry a confidence field, the quant desk publishes P10/P90 bands and a probability of upside for every name, the regime model reports its own posterior, and the research copilot grades its own grounding. The machine holds a precise opinion of how much it should be believed.

The identity is what happens when the screen stops keeping that a secret. Certainty becomes visible. Doubt becomes visible. Nothing on the screen asks to be believed — it asks to be checked.

A number you can't trace is a rumor with good posture. UAA doesn't print rumors.

fact: quote, filing, close.
derived: composite.ts, scoring.ts.
conjecture: forecast.py, monte_carlo.py, every AI verdict.

the taxonomy already exists in the code. the brand only stops hiding it.
§2 · INK

Four inks. Not a palette — a law.

Color in UAA is not chosen; it is earned. Text and numbers are printed at one of four strengths, and the strength is set by epistemology, not by hierarchy, taste, or emphasis.

INK I · FACT 247,318.42 Observed. A close, a filing, a fill. Full strength, always.
INK II · DERIVED 247,318.42 Computed from facts by a pure function. Strong, one step from the source.
INK III · CONJECTURE 247,318.42 A forecast, a fitted value, a model's belief. Printed lighter because it is lighter.
PENCIL composite.ts · quality 28 / value 24 / growth 24 · refreshed 09:42:07 The machine's own working. Never a conclusion. Always checkable.

The one law of ink: the machine may not print what it believes as dark as what it observed. There is no override, no exception for marketing, no “emphasis.” If a designer wants something darker, the engine has to know it better. Ink strength stops being a style decision and becomes a claim the interface is making — one it can be held to.

Green and red keep their old jobs and stay out of everyone else's: gains and losses, nothing more. There is no brand accent. The absence of a hero color is the color system. What a competitor would spend on a gradient, UAA spends on being legible about doubt.

same number, four confidences.
reflow: none. only the ink moves.

faint is relative. it is never illegible — that would be a different kind of lie.
§3 · PENCIL

The blue the camera couldn't see

Print shops kept a special blue — non-photo blue — for ruling margins and marking corrections. The process camera couldn't register it, so the working stayed on the paste-up and never reached the printed page. Every published page in the twentieth century sat on top of a layer of blue thinking that the reader was never allowed to see.

UAA keeps the blue and prints it anyway. The pencil layer is the machine's own working — which engine, which weights, which timestamp, which cache — ruled into a margin beside every judgment. Other software buries provenance in a tooltip and calls it clean. UAA rules a margin for it and calls it honest.

The pencil never concludes. It cites, dates, attributes, and qualifies. It is the only voice on screen allowed to talk about the software itself — and the only color on screen that isn't ink or the market.

non-photo blue, set here at #8FC7E8 on carbon.

in print it marked what the camera must not see.
here it marks what you must.

one blue, one job. it never touches a button, a link, or a headline.

§5 · MARKS

The proofreader's toolbox

The shape language comes from the desk where documents were checked, not the studio where they were styled. Four marks, each with one job, none decorative. Corners are square because sheets of paper are.

‸

The caret — where you act

The proofreader's insertion mark. In UAA it points at the one place on a surface where your hand is wanted. Every primary action wears it; nothing else may.

† ‡

The dagger — where the proof lives

Claims carry daggers; daggers carry you to the working. An AI sentence without a dagger is a sentence the grounding engine could not stand behind — and it prints in Ink III to say so.

Margins compressed for the sixth straight quarter† while guidance held.‡

The hatch — where we are not sure

Engravers shaded doubt with parallel strokes; drafters hatched the section they'd cut open. UAA hatches uncertainty — forecast fans, low-coverage data, degraded signals. Texture, never fog: a solid fill would claim a confidence the model doesn't have.

stet

Stet — let it stand

The proofreader's verdict on a marked passage: reviewed, and kept. The decision journal borrows it whole. When you re-examine a position and choose not to act, that is not nothing — it is stet, dated and of record.

The diamond survives, demoted from logo to punctuation: the loading mark still resolves square-to-diamond, because a diamond is a caret that made up its mind — two carets, point to point. Work pending is an open square; work concluded, a set diamond. The mark you already shipped was this identity's first sentence.

§6 · CHARTS

The past in ink. The future in pencil.

A chart is a sentence about time, and UAA conjugates it honestly: what happened is drawn in ink, what is computed from it in thinner ink, and what is merely expected in pencil — dashed, hatched, and exactly as light as its odds.

NVDA · TRAILING YEAR, FORWARD QUARTER · forecast.py · P10–P90 · p(up) 0.63
happened — ink expected — pencil, dashed P10–P90 — hatched ◆ last observed close

No gradient under the line — a gradient is an opinion about mood. No glow — light is not evidence. The rule generalizes to every surface: backtests are pencil until they're walked forward, fitted values are pencil laid over inked actuals, and a signal the engine itself flags as degraded drops to hatched pencil in the conviction book. Tear the labels off a UAA chart and the epistemology is still legible. That is the screenshot test, passed at the level of meaning instead of styling.

the band is the engine's own P10–P90, not an artist's ribbon.

hatch: 45°, 1px, pencil-blue.
median: dashed — a dash is a line that admits gaps.

the last observed point is the only diamond: the working, set.

§8 · HOUSE STYLE

Written like the working papers of a careful desk

The voice is not "institutional." It is a specific person: the analyst who checks their own arithmetic and expects you to check it too. Dry. Exact. Unhurried. Occasionally — when the facts permit — funny.

i

Verdict first, working after. The reader's time is the scarcest asset under management.

ii

Numbers are exact or absent. "About 40 seconds on a local model" is exact about its own precision. "Blazing fast" is a number that lost its nerve.

iii

Every sentence must survive the question "says who?" If the answer isn't a dagger, an engine, or a timestamp, the sentence prints in Ink III or not at all.

iv

No exclamation marks. No apologies. No cheer. The reader is a professional doing dangerous arithmetic with their own money; the correct register is the one used around loaded instruments.

v

Teach in passing or not at all. A caption that merely describes is blank space with better posture.

§10 · VERDICT

What this costs, and what it buys

†

Faint ink will be called low contrast. The floor is 4.5:1 and nothing meaningful ever sits below it — but the objection will come, and the answer must be the philosophy, said plainly: the lightness is information. If that answer embarrasses us, the identity is wrong.

†

The ink law demands engineering. Every rendered figure needs a provenance class — fact, derived, conjecture. Most of the plumbing exists (the score kinds, the confidence fields, the grounding engine); the discipline of wiring ink to it does not, yet. This identity cannot be applied by a stylesheet alone. That is precisely why it cannot be stolen by one.

†

No hero color means no cheap recognition. UAA gives up the thing a brand guideline usually leads with. In exchange, its recognition runs deeper than hue: the hatching, the margins, the one-word serif, the numbers that disagree about how dark to be. A competitor can copy any one of these in an afternoon. To copy all of them, they would have to mean it.

†

The wit must be rationed. "You are watching nothing. That is rarely deliberate" works once per screen, not once per sentence. The default register is dry; the joke is the exception that proves the desk is human.

Other terminals dress certainty up. This one undresses it — and turns out to be the only one with nothing to hide.
