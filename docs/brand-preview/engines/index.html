IDENTITY PROPOSAL III · SHOWN INSIDE THE PRODUCT, NOT BESIDE IT

Never one number.

UAA computes six different answers about every asset, from six engines that don't share a method. Most products average them into one score, because one score is easy to sell. UAA keeps them apart, because the disagreement is the information. When the factor model won't endorse what the fundamentals love, that gap is the most useful thing on the screen. The whole identity is built to show it.

This was in the codebase before it was a brand

Nothing here is imported from a mood board. The product already names every score by the question it answers, already lets engines abstain when they have no basis, already refuses to color a fit score as a Buy. The identity work is making that visible at reading speed.

lib/score-kinds.ts · line 131 "…They are different questions, so they can disagree."

Who's answering

Six engines and one analyst. Each engine owns one question and one mark. The marks are small on purpose — they live inside dense tables, not on posters.

The rail

Every score sits on the same 0–100 rail, with ticks where a call actually changes — 25, 42, 60, 78, the product's real recommendation bands. Same geometry at every size, from a page header to a table cell, so your eye learns it once and reads it forever. The page's own engine prints in ink; visiting engines print in their color. You always know who's speaking.

C 81 S 86 Q 62 F 58 0100

Fit and Quality rails carry no band ticks and no verdict colors — they aren't Buy/Sell questions, and the interface refuses to imply they are.

When engines split, the product says so

A spread wider than twenty points between banded engines prints a split chip — SPLIT 24 — wherever the score appears. It isn't an error state. It's the product doing its job: a split is a reason to read before you act, and UAA is the only product that will hand you one.

Why nobody can copy it

Any team can copy the rail in an afternoon. Filling it is the hard part: it takes six engines that genuinely answer different questions, an AI disciplined enough to explain without voting, and the nerve to let your own product disagree with itself in public. That last part is the moat. Every competitor's brand promises certainty. UAA's brand is that certainty has to be earned in front of you.
